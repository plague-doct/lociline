✎ Prototype notes for capstone review
This build replaces the puzzle rooms with a walking piece. Act I is five corridors. You walk them on rails — the camera carries you forward and stops itself wherever there's something to think about — and at each fork you choose which way to go. The branch you don't take stays faintly visible on the floor, so you can see the reading you didn't do.

The quiz is now one question at the door. It only checks you've read the scene before you're allowed to reflect on it. It is not scored, not recorded, and has nothing to do with the artifact. A single question is a speed bump rather than a real lock — a determined student can guess it — which is a deliberate trade for making the room itself reflective rather than testing.

The artifact is woven from the walk, not from the writing. Which stations you stopped at, which fork you chose, how long you lingered, which moments you answered at all, and whether you marked a thought as coming from your own reading or from class discussion — that's structured data, and that's all the AI ever touches. Your actual sentences are never read, parsed or summarised by anything. They sit beside the artifact in your own words, for you and your teacher to talk about.

Welcome

Reading, with a thinking partner

Before you begin, here's exactly what's happening on this page and what isn't.

✎

Nothing you write is read by the AI

What you type at each stop stays exactly as you typed it. No AI reads it, changes it, scores it or summarises it. At the end you'll see your own sentences, word for word.

⚙

The AI only maps where you walked

It knows which way you turned, where you stopped, and how long you stayed. It turns that into the picture at the end — never your words, never what you meant.

🔒

One question, then it's yours

There's a single question at each door to check you've read the scene. It isn't marked and it isn't saved. Get it wrong as many times as you like — it just sends you back to the text.

Note for teachers and parents: there is no open chat with the AI anywhere in this experience, and no student writing is sent to a model. The door questions are recall checks on the text and are not recorded or reported. The end-of-session map is generated from navigation data only. Written responses are shown back verbatim and are designed to be read aloud in class discussion.

What you wrote is not in that list

Your sentences are kept and shown back to you word for word, but nothing reads them. The picture at the end of a book is built only from the things above — where you walked and what you tapped.

⚙

The AI is a translator, not an author

It turns those choices into shapes and colours. It never decides what you think, and it never writes anything for you.

🔒

Nothing here is marked

The question at each door isn't scored or saved. There are no points and no leaderboards. The only thing kept is what you chose to say.

What the AI used, exactly: the forks you chose, the stops you made, how long you stayed at each, the themes you tapped, and whether you marked each one as coming from reading, from class, or from somewhere else. That's the whole list — five things you chose from fixed options, and one clock. It never opened a single sentence you wrote, and it never read where you said your somewhere-else came from: those are printed below untouched, and the picture above would look identical whatever you'd typed into them.

What you said, in your words

Why it's built this way: a picture generated from a child's sentences is a machine deciding what those sentences meant. A picture generated from their footsteps is a record of a choice they actually made. The second one can be argued with in class — you can point at a fork and say why you went left. Your tapestry on the bookshelf is woven from the same walking data, and grows denser each time you come back to Act I.
