The teacher's side

Your class, your questions

Every stop in every room asks one question. This is where you replace any of them with your own — and where you read what your class wrote back, word for word, with nothing in between.

Whatever you call the class. It labels the prompts you write and the walks that come back, so two classes on the same device don't get mixed up.

One question stands at each stop. Write over any of them and your class sees yours instead — they are never told it changed, because either way the question is the one their teacher set. Leave a box empty to go back to the original.

This is the only place any of this is read. Nothing a student writes is sent anywhere, scored, summarised or shown to an AI — not in the tool, and not here. It is printed below exactly as they typed it.

Each child walks under a pen name, the name of a writer or a character from a book, so you can see who did what without this tool ever holding a real name. Deal one per child, print the slips, and write the child's name on the paper slip. Keep the slips yourself. The child picks their pen name from a list on the bookshelf; they can't type one, so a real name can't go in by mistake.

Where this lives. Prompts and walks are kept in this browser, on this device, and go nowhere else. There is no account and no server in this prototype: a class set of responses is whatever was walked on this machine. Student logins and a real class roll are the next piece of work, not this one.
